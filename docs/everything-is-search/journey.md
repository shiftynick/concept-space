# Website and documentary journey

This is a proposed learning sequence, not a locked chapter list. The site and film should share an intellectual progression while using their media differently.

## Provisional website modules

### 1. A space of possibilities

Question: how can something that has not happened have a place in a diagram?

Start with a small 2D invented world. Define what a location means. Establish that the picture represents alternatives, not necessarily physical space. The viewer should be able to distinguish one possibility from the collection of alternatives.

Optional: briefly begin on a line if it makes the first allowed change obvious. Do not add a dimensional prelude merely for visual spectacle.

### 2. Possible does not mean accessible

Question: why might a possibility exist in the picture but remain out of reach?

Introduce an initial state and allowed moves. Show a reachable region within the broader frame. Change one rule. Let the viewer identify what becomes accessible and what stays unavailable.

This module is where the outer/inner boundary interpretation must be settled in plain language.

### 3. Exploration without a destination

Question: what happens if we remove the target?

Run a simple process under explicit toy rules. Show current position, history, and alternatives separately. The point is to make target-free exploration conceivable, not to imply that randomness defines all search.

Compare more than one allowed exploration rule. Ask what a rule makes common, rare, or inaccessible.

### 4. What remains?

Question: what does it mean for something to stick around?

Keep this module open until the creator's intuition is better understood. Possible toy contrasts include a trace that fades, a state that persists, a pattern that recurs, or a structure that can be copied. These are different mechanisms, not interchangeable definitions of stability.

A useful viewer outcome would be: “I need to ask what persistence means in this particular example.”

### 5. History changes what happens next

Question: can the route already taken affect future exploration?

Introduce one history-dependent rule. Compare runs from the same start, then inspect the consequences of an early difference. A later extension could let an accumulated result change the allowed moves themselves.

Distinguish different routes through a fixed space from changes to the accessible space.

### 6. More dimensions, less literal geography

Question: what have our simple pictures left out?

Build the 2D scene into 3D by adding a meaningful independent variable. Show that additional distinctions could still remain unseen. State what distance and overlap mean in this particular visualization.

Placement is provisional: this may belong between modules 2 and 3 if it improves the flow. The pedagogical reason should determine the reveal.

### 7. A deliberately goal-directed example

Question: what changes when we really are searching *for* something?

A small design or puzzle task could introduce a destination or explicit evaluation criterion. Contrast it with the earlier target-free process. This earns the distinction between a broad exploration lens and the narrower case of optimization.

### 8. Model training

Question: what is being varied, constrained, and retained here?

Use Concept Space as a reference, but resolve a crucial mapping before writing the script: are we depicting possible model configurations, possible behaviors, or concepts reachable by a trained model? They should not be presented as one literal space.

**Proposed bridge:** one view shows changes to a model configuration; a paired view shows an illustrative change in conceptual reach. The relationship requires technical research and careful labeling. Keep training, inference, and prompting as distinct candidate processes.

### 9. Evolution

Question: can this lens describe change without implying foresight or an intended destination?

Define the represented objects and allowed changes before choosing a visual. Candidate spaces include traits, behaviors, or configurations; these choices are not equivalent. Research how to depict inheritance, variation, environments, and persistence without reducing the example to one walker pursuing a peak.

This module must earn its biological claims through sources and expert review. Its place here is a research proposal.

### 10. Culture and collective exploration

Question: what changes when possibilities are shared, interpreted, preserved, and transformed by many people?

Potential examples include tools, practices, language, or artistic forms. Choose one concrete case rather than explaining all culture at once. Investigate what counts as an alternative, a transmission, a transformation, and persistence in that case.

Concept Space's individual/society/external-memory distinction is a useful starting reference. Avoid making society a single mind or treating popularity as proof of truth or value.

### 11. Searches interacting

Question: what if the results of one process change the conditions for another?

Candidate combinations: tools affecting exploration, records affecting what can be revisited, or multiple processes changing one another's constraints. Use only relationships we can explain and support. This is a promising advanced module, not a commitment to a universal causal diagram.

### 12. Does everything become search?

Return to the title. Show what the lens made visible, which meanings changed between examples, and where the analogy strained.

Invite the viewer to apply the questions to an example of their own. Include at least one difficult case in development: a simple repeating process, passive relaxation, or an event with no obvious retained history. We have not decided whether these qualify as search; the point is to test the definition rather than protect it.

## Candidate examples to evaluate

These are brainstorm candidates, not researched recommendations or settled scientific analogies.

| Candidate | Potential teaching value | Main question before inclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Invented rules on a grid | Completely inspectable possibilities and constraints | Does it express exploration without quietly installing a goal? |
| Making a melody | Alternatives, constraints, and human choices | Is the important selection mechanism the maker, listener, or both? |
| Designing a simple object | Explicit goals alongside unexpected alternatives | Does this make the broad lens seem too goal-dependent? |
| Trying to explain an idea | Shared reach and different interpretations | What exactly changes: attention, understanding, or access? |
| Training a model | Connection to the existing prototype | Which space is changing, and how is it related to conceptual reach? |
| Biological evolution | A demanding non-intentional example | What objects and mechanisms can be represented faithfully? |
| Cultural practice or tool lineage | Collective change and preservation | What concrete, documented case avoids a sweeping claim? |
| Scientific inquiry | Exploration shaped by methods and accumulated records | How do we preserve the distinction between surviving claims and warranted claims? |

## A shared module template

For each module, draft:

1. One question the viewer brings in.
2. The example and what each mark represents.
3. One change to observe or control.
4. What should be directly visible, versus explained in words.
5. One sentence the viewer should be able to say afterward.
6. One tempting misreading to test.
7. What is invented, what is evidenced, and what remains disputed.
8. A film version and an interactive version.

## Documentary direction

Creator direction: documentary style, covering the same ideas, using visualizations developed for the website. `~/Work/motion-mania` may support production later.

**Proposal:** treat the film as a composed argument and the site as a place to inspect and vary its examples. A film should not be a screen recording of someone operating controls.

A provisional three-act structure:

- **Act I — Seeing alternatives:** introduce possibility, accessibility, and exploration without a target through one simple world.
- **Act II — Recognizing patterns and differences:** develop concrete examples, revisiting the same questions while preserving distinct mechanisms.
- **Act III — Testing the lens:** examine interacting searches, changing conditions, and the limits of the title's claim.

Do not set a runtime until the intended audience, number of examples, and depth are clearer. Likewise, voice, music, interview material, documentary evidence, and narration style remain open.

### Shared production foundation, when implementation begins

- Repeatable scene states and repeatable random seeds where randomness is used.
- A timeline that can be scrubbed independently of wall-clock time.
- Camera and label layouts suitable for both interaction and composed shots.
- Scene descriptions that separate claims, visual events, and narration.
- Explicit records of synthetic illustrations versus sourced evidence.
- Exportable clean frames or sequences without web controls.
- Separate pacing: the website waits for a person; the film supplies its own rhythm.

These are future requirements to investigate. No capability of the current renderer or Motion Mania has been assumed or verified here.

## Anchor-led development order — creator clarification, 2026-10-13

The creator's first associations are biological evolution, human cultural/societal evolution at micro and macro levels, and neural-network training. Prioritize those three in conceptual development and eventual research. Other examples remain optional explanatory aids.

Distinguish development order from presentation order: we can clarify the thesis through these anchors first, then design a simpler opening that prepares viewers for them.

**Proposed recurring visual device:** inspect a local change, pull back to many changes over time, then ask what becomes visible at the larger scale. Candidate pairings to investigate include a local biological variation versus a population or lineage history; one person's adoption or modification of a practice versus a broader cultural pattern; and a model update versus a training trajectory. These are scene proposals, not established equivalents. Define the represented objects and timescale separately in each case.

## Revised narrative spine: meet a possibility before seeing the space

Creator endorsement: show very fine-grained tangible search progress, then zoom outward. Use the adjacent possible as a recurring question: **what can happen next from here, and how does a change affect that answer?**

Assistant proposal: develop one complete local-to-wide sequence for each anchor before deciding the full chapter count. Each sequence should preserve the identity of at least one event throughout the zoom.

Candidate close-ups for discussion, not selected or researched examples:

- **Culture:** a person modifies a small tool or a musical phrase; show a few available modifications, then widen to adaptations by other people and changes across time. A fictional example must be labeled as such; a historical lineage requires evidence.
- **Life:** choose a specific, sourceable biological change whose immediate context can be explained accurately, then widen to population or lineage dynamics. Do not invent a smooth mutation-to-trait-to-success story merely because it animates well.
- **Neural-network training:** start with a tiny, inspectable model changing its response to a simple example; show the relevant update, then widen to a training trajectory. Keep parameter changes, output behavior, and conceptual reach visibly distinct.

For the film, compose the zoom as a continuous explanatory passage. For the website, let the viewer pause at each scale, inspect the concrete event, and return from the overview. No final choice of close-up, scale controls, or film shots is implied yet.
