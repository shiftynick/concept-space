# Vision and central questions

## Creator direction

The initiating intuition is: **“everything is search,” or at least can be framed as search.** Model training, evolution, culture, and other processes may be understood as exploration within spaces of concepts, ideas, or possibilities.

The creator explicitly questions whether there is a “for” in this search. The purpose is not to retrofit every process into pursuit of a chosen destination. Something unfolds or explores, and some things stick around. Exactly what makes something persist is unresolved and may differ fundamentally between examples.

Two connected works are envisioned:

- An interactive website that builds the idea from scratch through progressively richer modules.
- A documentary-style film that develops the same ideas through narration and visual sequences drawn from the website's visualization system.

Concept Space supplies a reference: a surrounding space of possibilities, irregular internal reach, holes, density, growth, comparison, and cinematic narrative. The new work expands the subject beyond models and beyond describing static coverage.

## Proposed framing, not yet agreed

A possible opening sentence:

> What if we looked at the world as possibilities being explored—and asked what makes some of them persist?

A more specific working formulation:

> Many different processes can be examined through the possibilities available to them, the changes they can make, the paths they actually take, and what remains of those paths.

The first is an invitation. The second supplies questions that might make the invitation useful. Neither establishes that every process is literally the same kind of search.

“Everything Is Search” can remain the provocative title while the work earns, qualifies, or revises it through examples. Whether the final voice should advocate the thesis, investigate it, or combine both remains open.

## The main conceptual move

Concept Space currently emphasizes **what can be reached**. This new direction adds **what happens through time**, and potentially **how that history changes future possibilities**.

A candidate recurring sequence is:

1. Establish the possibilities relevant to this example.
2. Show which changes are available under its present conditions.
3. Let the process unfold, without assuming a named destination.
4. Observe what disappears, recurs, accumulates, or persists.
5. Ask whether what happened changes the next set of possibilities.

This sequence is an organizing proposal, not a requirement that every example contain each stage.

## Distinctions worth preserving

### Exploration and optimization

A goal-directed search can be one special example. It should not define the entire project. Some examples may have explicit criteria; others may have local feedback, changing conditions, or no useful single objective. Do not draw a target or summit unless that example calls for one.

### Possibility and history

What has happened is a subset of what could happen. A point appearing for the first time need not mean the possibility itself has just been created. Conversely, we may eventually want examples in which the rules or available components change. Those are different claims and should look different.

### Persistence and value

“Sticks around” does not automatically mean better, truer, more intelligent, more desirable, or globally optimal. We have not agreed on a shared persistence mechanism. Ask what persistence means in each scene before drawing it.

### Concepts and possibilities

Open: is “concept” the right umbrella for every example? For some scenes the object might be an idea; in others it might be a configuration, behavior, design, or state. **Proposal:** use “possibility space” as the broad visual vocabulary and “concept space” for examples where it is specifically appropriate. This is not a proposed repository rename.

### One space and many spaces

Open: do all examples inhabit one universal space, or does each example define its own useful space? Starting with a space specific to each example avoids silently claiming that a model's parameters, a cultural practice, and an organism's traits share literal coordinates. A universal interpretation can remain a later question.

## What would make the lens useful?

The experience should help someone notice a distinction they previously missed: possible versus accessible, visited versus retained, local change versus distant possibility, repeated exploration versus new territory, or constraints that change during a process.

It should also leave room for failure. If every change qualifies as search, what explanatory work is the word doing? A strong version of this project should show where the lens illuminates something and where it becomes too broad or obscures an important difference.

## Desired experience

The creator's visual ambition remains high. The work should invite sustained curiosity, with striking motion and clearly connected words. Beauty should make the idea easier to attend to; the viewer should also be able to say what changed and why the example matters.

Proposed emotional progression: curiosity → recognition → surprise at a connection → productive doubt → a new way of looking. This is a creative suggestion, not a settled tone.

## Out of scope for this draft

We are not claiming a scientific theory, asserting that evolution or culture has intentions, presenting synthetic geometry as measured data, or selecting a final production stack. The mathematical model, evidence base, audience, film length, site structure, and final thesis strength remain open.

## Creator's anchor examples — 2026-10-13

When asked which examples most immediately evoke the intuition, the creator named:

- Evolution of life.
- Evolution of human culture and society.
- Neural-network model training.

Micro and macro levels matter, explicitly in the evolution examples. These are the conceptual anchors, even if a simpler invented world remains the best teaching introduction. They should guide discovery rather than be treated as interchangeable entries in a broad example catalogue.

### Proposed next comparison

Ask the same questions of each anchor, allowing different answers and failures of fit:

- What are the possibilities in this view?
- What changes locally, and what pattern do we see over a longer period or across many interacting parts?
- What makes some changes available and others unavailable?
- What, if anything, persists—and by what mechanism in this particular example?
- Does what persists affect subsequent exploration?

A possible common thread to test with the creator is **history influencing which possibilities come next**. This is an assistant hypothesis about the intuition, not a confirmed definition of search or persistence.

Do not assume that microscopic change is simply a miniature version of the macro process. Likewise, do not assume a one-to-one mapping between biological inheritance, cultural transmission, and changes to neural-network parameters. The similarities and the differences both belong in the investigation.

## The adjacent possible — creator clarification, 2026-10-13

The creator strongly endorsed the formulation “What happens now changes which possibilities can be explored next,” connecting it to the phrase **“the adjacent possible,”** which they remember their late friend Jim Rutt using. Preserve this personal association. This records the creator's recollection; it does not attribute the term's origin or a particular published definition to Jim. Any public quotation, historical attribution, or biographical treatment requires separate sourcing and editorial agreement.

The creator also endorsed showing **very fine-grained, tangible examples of search progress and exploration, then zooming outward**. This becomes a central storytelling direction rather than an optional visual flourish.

The adjacent possible is now a promising organizing idea. Its exact meaning still needs definition within each example. In particular, this endorsement does not settle whether possibilities are newly created or become newly accessible, nor does it settle the outer boundary or a universal persistence mechanism.

Assistant proposal for the narrative spine: begin with a concrete present situation, reveal some changes available from it, follow one change, and show how the next available changes differ. Then widen the view to reveal how many such local events form a larger history.
