# Discovery process and decision log

Our goal is to get closer to the creator's nebulous idea, not to substitute a polished assistant-authored theory for it.

## Proposed way of working

### Round 1 — Find the intuition's center

Use one concrete example and ask what makes it feel like search. Draft two or three short alternative descriptions. The creator can react to phrases that feel right, wrong, or too narrow.

Prefer a small number of questions per exchange. Keep ambiguity when it is productive; do not turn every open point into an immediate decision.

### Round 2 — Contrast two examples

Choose one intuitive case and one case with a substantially different mechanism. Fill in: possibilities, available changes, constraints, actual activity, and what persists. Mark any category that does not fit.

The question is whether the lens carries something useful between cases, not whether their tables can be made superficially identical.

### Round 3 — Establish the visual grammar

Agree on what the outer frame, inner region, moving marks, and traces mean. Start with a rough 2D sketch. Only then test animation and the 3D reveal.

Ask the creator to describe the scene back in their own words. If the description differs from the intended claim, revise the diagram or the claim.

### Round 4 — Research the selected examples

Conduct focused research after choosing examples and deciding what each is meant to illustrate. Prefer primary research, original technical documentation, and authoritative domain sources. Record disagreements and counterexamples alongside supporting material.

Research questions include goal-directed versus open-ended processes; reachable versus practically accessible possibilities; history-dependent exploration; distinct persistence mechanisms; and what, if anything, is gained by a shared account of training, evolution, and culture.

For each factual claim, keep its source, qualifications, and the exact passage or visual it supports. Do not assemble citations merely to validate the title.

### Round 5 — Develop a small complete sequence

Create one module in enough detail to test: narrative, visual changes, interaction, and a film-style shot outline. Review it before committing to the entire website or documentary.

### Round 6 — Expand and test

Build the wider sequence around what worked. Test with new viewers; ask what they think each visual means before revealing the intended interpretation. Independent model critique can supplement this, as in the Concept Space review, but cannot replace viewer evidence.

## Highest-leverage unresolved questions

These are a backlog, not a questionnaire to answer all at once.

1. What is the clearest everyday example of the creator's intuition?
2. What makes a process count as search rather than merely change? Is that distinction important to the creator?
3. Is the outer space defined separately for each example, or is there an intended deeper universal space?
4. Does “growing search” primarily mean accumulating history, expanding reach, persisting results, or different things in different scenes?
5. When conditions change, are possibilities newly created or newly accessible—or do we need both accounts?
6. What makes the title useful even if it is not literally universal?
7. Who should the opening work for: a curious general audience, an AI-interested audience, or another group?
8. Should the documentary feel like a personal investigation, an explanatory essay, or a thesis being argued?
9. Which present Concept Space scenes feel closest to the new idea, and which now feel misleading?
10. What should viewers notice in their own lives afterward?
